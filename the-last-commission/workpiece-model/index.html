Summary

The Workpiece Model is the one shared record of every piece of metal being worked: its parts, cells, temperatures, shape, surface, defects and runes. It has no behaviour; each data channel has exactly one writer system, and the defect log is append-only, so nothing done to the metal is lost or quietly changed. Heat, Deformation, Blood Runes, Grading, saves and Living Armor all read this record instead of keeping their own copies.

Quick reference
Layer Foundation · Priority MVP · Key deps None. Depended on by Heat, Craft-Step Framework, Bar/Sheet Deformation, Grading, Blood Runes, Horror Event Director, Recipes, Living Armor, saves, Mail Weaving.
Read this document by its four ideas
  • One record. The metal exists in exactly one place; nobody keeps a private copy.
  • One writer per channel. Every value has an owner; everyone else reads.
  • Nothing is erased. Defects are appended, repairs are new entries, history stays.
  • A fixed order each step. Input, then deformation, then heat, then the grading sample.
01

Overview

The Workpiece Model is the single source of truth for every piece of metal the smith works on: the seax on the anvil, the helm on the raising stake, the rivet going into a pauldron. It is a pure data layer with no behaviour of its own. It describes what a workpiece is (its parts, their shape, temperature, hardness, defects and inscriptions) and lets other systems change it under strict ownership rules: every data channel has exactly one system allowed to write it, and everyone else only reads.

The player never touches this system directly. They touch what it makes possible. Every hammer strike, every heat, every quench and every blood rune leaves its result here, which is why a bar that was struck too cold stays cracked and a helm that was raised without annealing stays brittle. Without it, Heat Simulation, the two deformation systems and Grading would each keep their own copy of the metal and drift apart. With it, one model is read by Grading (to judge the piece), by saves (to restore it mid-night), and by every future item, because a weapon and a suit of armor are the same data shaped differently.

The model also fixes the order in which each frame changes the metal: the player's verb input first, then deformation, then heat, then the grading sample. How the model is stored and transported in Unity (component, ScriptableObject, native buffers) is an implementation choice and becomes an ADR; this document specifies behaviour and data only.

02

Player Fantasy

Nothing you do to the metal is forgotten.

Indirect: the player feels the effects, never the system.

The steel is honest. It shows you exactly what your hands did: the even taper you drew, the burn where you held it in too long, the crack you hammered into it cold. You can read every piece like a ledger of the night. But the ledger keeps more than your hammer. When you turned from the work to look at the door, the metal kept that too: a mark you did not strike, a flaw that came from nowhere you can name. You cannot hide a mistake from the metal, and you cannot hide what you ignored.
EmotionAccountable intimacy that turns into unease. The craftsman's comfort is that the metal is honest; the horror is that it is too honest. It keeps his devotion and his neglect, and forgets neither.
Anchor momentLifting the piece from the quench and turning it in the lamplight. The even hammer marks are his. The faint scale burn is from when he let it sit too long. Near the tang there is a hairline mark he does not remember making, left during the minute he turned to look at the door. No UI, no score, no jump: the player recognises their own competence, and then something in the record is not theirs.
Design testPlaytesters, unprompted, inspect finished pieces before handing them over, and some describe a mark as "not mine". If they talk about a score or a corruption level instead, the presentation layers have leaked.

Pillars served

  • The Hands Remember: the metal remembers the hands, stroke by stroke.
  • Perfection Is the Price: this record is what gets judged, and the verdict is never shown directly.
  • The Metal Won't Wait: an ignored horror event leaves a physical mark in the piece, so the dilemma has a consequence the player can hold.
  • Dread, Not Fright: the strange mark is never explained.
Tone and language (inherited by Grading, Order Sheet UI and saves)
Plain craftsman's vocabulary: heat, colour, grain, scale, temper, true, cold-shut, a crack that "sings" under the hammer. The metal holds, keeps, carries, remembers. Never "cursed", "haunted" or "evil", and never gauge words ("durability", "stats", "quality score", "corruption meter"), which would break the indirect framing and Pillar 4's rule against announcing perfection.

Shaped with creative-director (framing B of three; A "The Metal Remembers Your Hands" and C "It Keeps Something For Him" rejected: A leaves the dread entirely to other systems, C explains the entity and turns the metal against the player).

03

Detailed Design

03.1 · Core rules 1–4

Structure

  1. 1

    Workpiece. A Workpiece is one item in progress (a seax; a helm and its rivets). It owns parts[], joints[], one defect_log, a lifecycle state, a recipe_target ID and a monotonic tick. It is not a world object: it has no transform, mesh or station.

  2. 2

    Part. A Part is one piece of material with an immutable part_id, a kind, a material_id (a data reference: critical temperatures, conductivity, burn point) and an item_role (the recipe slot it fills). Kinds come from a data-defined PartKind registry: Bar and Plate at Ship; Mail Weaving adds a ring-graph kind later through the same extension point.

  3. 3

    Discretisation.

    • A Bar is an ordered strip of segments along a spine, each with a stable seg_id and prev/next adjacency.
    • A Plate is an R_u × R_v grid of regions with 4-neighbour adjacency.
    • The count is fixed for a Part's life and sized from the longer of the recipe's finished dimensions and the stock's: N = ceil(max(target, stock) / bar_seg_target) for bars, and likewise per axis for plates (Formulas 1–2). Plate extents are measured over the finished surface, not its flat projection. A 40 cm seax gets 40 segments while it is still a short ingot; they start dense and reach about 1 cm when the blade is drawn to length. Segment length (bar) and region area (plate) are channels, so drawing out changes length, never count.
    • Caps: 64 segments per bar, 24 × 24 regions per plate, 16 parts per Workpiece. Rivets are joint attributes, not parts.
    • The floors (4 segments, 2 regions per axis) apply at Create only. A split may leave a part with as few as 1 cell (Edge Cases); its count is then fixed like any other.
    • Each Part exposes a local parametric frame (bar: u along the spine, plus face; plate: u, v). The model provides the lookup from a frame coordinate to a segment or region ID. Gameplay never reads meshes.
  4. 4

    Pure data. The model holds plain, serialisable values: no engine objects, references or callbacks, and no clock. The frame orchestrator (owned by the Craft-Step Framework) advances tick once per simulation step. (How it is stored in Unity becomes an ADR; requirement: it must be testable without the engine running.)

Why size from the longer of stock and target? A short ingot drawn into a long blade would otherwise end with coarse segments; a long stock cut down would end with segments finer than needed. Taking the longer keeps every finished cell at about 1 cm (bar) or 2 cm (plate) or finer.

Why is the count fixed? Runes, defects and repairs point at stable cell IDs. A count that changed during forging would move them, and the record would no longer match the metal.

Faces
A bar has two faces (A, B); a plate has outer and inner. Grinding one face never changes another face's surface channels or the runes on it. Rune samples are anchored to stable seg_id/region_ids, so a cut never moves them; removed_at_cut records the face's material_removed when the stroke was cut, so legibility can be judged.
Heat-treatment history needed to judge a quench (cooling rate through the critical range, soak time) is stored in Heat-owned channels defined by the Heat Simulation GDD; rule 7 forbids keeping it privately.
  1. 6

    Derived values are never stored. The model computes them on read: work_hardening, hole (punched, or thickness at or below t_min), volume and mass, inscription_legible (stroke depth greater than the material ground away since the stroke was cut), and active_defects (the log folded with its repair entries). A hole made by thinning is derived, so Deformation never writes punched.

  2. 7

    Persistence rule. Any value that persists across frames and changes an outcome must be a model channel, never private system state. Systems may keep derived caches only. Soak timers, snapshots and "last seen" bookkeeping all live here, so saves and Grading are always complete.

  3. 8

    Write enforcement.

    • Every channel is tagged with its writer. A write from any other system is refused: in development builds it asserts and logs the channel, caller and tick; in shipping builds it is rejected and logged, never applied silently.
    • A writer may only write during its own phase (rule 15); an out-of-phase write is refused the same way.
    • Reads are unrestricted and return read-only views.
    • Writes apply immediately (no double buffer). Determinism comes from the fixed phase order plus single ownership.
  4. 9

    Cross-system effects: counters, applied by the owner. When one system's action changes another system's channel, the actor only advances its own monotonic counter, and the owner applies the effect to its own channel:

    • Anneal: Heat sets strain_at_last_anneal = strain_total on a full anneal, or blends toward it for a partial one. Deformation reads the difference as work_hardening.
    • Grinding: Deformation only increases material_removed (and changes its own geometry). Later in the same step, Heat consumes the difference from material_removed_seen and reduces scale_depth, decarb_depth and oxide_index itself. Grinding never removes burn_damage. Heat never changes thickness.
    • Hammering adds heat / thin metal cools faster: Heat reads the geometry and strike events and applies them to temperature itself. No other system writes temperature.
03.3 · Core rules 10–13

The defect log

  1. 10

    Append-only, one owner per kind. An entry is {id, kind, target (part_id + segment/region, or whole part), magnitude (in the writer's own unit), source, tick, step_id, payload}. Kind namespaces are owned:

    OwnerDefect kinds
    Heatburn, overheat, quench_crack
    Deformationcold_crack, over_strain, tear
    Craft-Step Frameworkmisfit, bad_cut, rivet_fail
    Blood Runesrune_flaw
    Horror Event Directorignored_event: the mark the metal keeps of what the smith ignored (see Player Fantasy)

    The model refuses an append whose kind does not belong to the caller. Writers report facts in their own units; Grading owns all weights. Entries are never edited or deleted; order is (tick, phase, sequence). Cracks are only ever log entries, never a channel, because two systems can cause them.

  2. 11

    Repairs are new entries. The owning writer appends a repair entry that references a defect id, or an overflowed (kind, target) (for example, Deformation grinding out a shallow cold crack). Repairs are exempt from the log cap. Reheating never repairs a crack, and ignored_event can never be repaired by anyone. active_defects folds the log. A flawed piece always continues (Pillar 4); its history is never erased.

  3. 12

    Log cap. Each Workpiece keeps at most defect_log_cap entries. Past the cap, the model keeps the first entries and increments an overflow count per (kind, target), so Grading still sees the total.

  4. 13

    Discarded material. Entries on a part that becomes Scrapped stay in the log, but Grading and Living Armor ignore them. Cutting off a cracked end really does remove the flaw from the finished item: that is real smithing, and it is a recovery the player can choose.

    Except the mark that is not his
    When a part carrying an active ignored_event is Scrapped, the Horror Event Director appends a new ignored_event on the nearest surviving part (same magnitude, payload pointing to the original id). The model never moves an entry; the Director re-appends, in the event-delivery phase of the next step (scrapping happens in the topology phase, after event delivery; rule 15). If the whole Workpiece is Scrapped for new stock, the mark is appended on the new Workpiece. You cannot cut away what you ignored (Pillar 3; user ruling 2026-10-31).
03.4 · Core rules 14–19

Operations and timing

  1. 14

    Structural operations are atomic and belong to the Craft-Step Framework. They are model operations, not channel writes:

    • Create: from a stock spec in recipe data (material, dimensions, initial temperature, target dimensions). A poured ingot starts hot through this path, not through a Heat write.
    • Split (cut): quantised to a segment or region boundary. Every channel value is carried into both halves; seg_ids stay stable.
    • Join / unjoin: a Joint is {part_a, anchor_a, part_b, anchor_b, kind (rivet | slot), slack}. Channels are never merged; seams stay part boundaries. Pose and articulation angles are not stored here: they are presentation, owned by Continuity and Living Armor.
  2. 15

    Frame order and read lag. Each simulation step runs, in order: event delivery (window events, Director appends) → verb input and Framework topology writes → Deformation → Heat → Grading sample. Writers that evolve over time (Heat, Deformation) must step at a fixed rate, independent of frame rate. The rates are owned by their GDDs.

ReaderSees
Deformation reading temperaturethe previous step
Heat reading geometrythe current step, after deformation
Gradingeverything, current step
  1. 16

    Change notification. State is polled; one-off happenings are events. Each channel carries a version counter per Workpiece, plus a dirty range for upload to the heat shader. The model emits exactly one event of its own: defect appended. Strike, quench and other happenings are emitted by the systems that cause them.

  2. 17

    Precision. Channels are stored at full precision, live and in saves, and a save restores them bit-for-bit. Small per-step changes (slow cooling near room temperature, fine grinding) must never be lost to rounding. Fixed quanta (1 °C; 0.01 mm for lengths and depths; 1e-10 m² for area) are used only for comparisons, display and recipe-derived counts (Formula 9). (Storage format and float determinism across machines become part of the storage ADR; only same-machine reproducibility is required.)

  3. 18

    Save hook. The model implements the save-participant interface owned by Night-Boundary Save: one snapshot with a schema version, all parts, joints, the defect log and tick. Restore is exact. Heat, Deformation and Grading never save model data themselves. Whether a piece keeps cooling across a resume is Mid-Night Resume's decision.

  4. 19

    What the model does not do. It holds no grading logic, no rendering and no pose. Continuity neither reads nor writes it; Living Armor reads it and sends requests to Continuity.

Edge Cases

Each case names the exact condition and the exact outcome. The coloured tag says how it resolves: refused nothing changes; assert a bug in a writer; allowed valid play; rule a defined behaviour.

Refusals
A refusal changes nothing, is logged (channel or operation, caller, tick, reason) and is reported to the caller. A refusal is never a defect: only a completed bad operation appends one.
Creation
If Any stock or target dimension is outside the Formula 1–2 input ranges (bar below 0.01 m, plate axis below 0.02 m, or above the maximum)Refused at CreateCreate is refused and the recipe data fails validation at load. This floor also guarantees Formula 1b's need for at least one length quantum per segment (0.01 m is 1 000 quanta, more than N_bar_max). A short part inside the range is valid: a 3 cm part gets N_bar_min segments.
Creation
If The recipe target and the stock differ so much that the count hits the cap (for example a 1.2 m stock for a 0.4 m blade)Valid, warnsValid. N clamps to N_bar_max, Formula 1b spreads the stock over N, and segments start longer than s_bar. Data validation warns at load; nothing fails at runtime.
Ownership & timing
If A system writes a channel it does not own, or writes outside its own phaseRefusedRefused (rule 8). Development builds assert; shipping builds refuse and log with the tick. The value is never applied.
Ownership & timing
If strain_at_last_anneal is greater than strain_total (a Heat bug)Assert + clampDevelopment builds assert. Shipping builds clamp it to strain_total on write. Formula 3's max(0, …) already protects readers.
Ownership & timing
If The grind delta Δ is negative (material_removed fell, which breaks Deformation's monotonic rule)Assert + resyncDevelopment builds assert. Heat treats Δ as 0 and sets material_removed_seen = material_removed, so the bug is not re-reported every step.
Ownership & timing
If Δ is larger than scale_depth, decarb_depth or d_oxide_clearClamp, no carryThe clamps in Formula 4 hold each value at 0. material_removed_seen still advances by the full Δ. Surplus grinding is never carried forward, so scale that grows back later is not "pre-ground".
Ownership & timing
If A frame lookup receives u or v that is NaN, infinite or outside 0–1No cellFormula 10 returns no cell, never a clamped or floored index; the caller treats the verb as refused. (Casting NaN to an integer gives an undefined index.)
Ownership & timing
If tick would overflowCannot happenIt is a 64-bit counter, so this cannot happen in play. Development builds assert above 2^53.
Defect log
If The log is at defect_log_cap and repairs keep arrivingBounded repairsRepairs are stored even past the cap (rule 11), but each is bounded. A repair is refused if its target id does not exist, if the defect's kind is not the caller's, or if it would make the repairs for an overflowed (kind, target) exceed its total. A second repair of an already repaired id is a logged no-op. So repairs can never outnumber defects, and the log cannot grow without limit. The check happens at append time; the fold in Formula 8 cannot detect a bad repair, only clamp it.
Defect log
If An entry targets a part that is later ScrappedKept, ignored · mark returnsThe entry stays in the log (rule 13); Grading and Living Armor skip it. Exception: an active ignored_event comes back. At the next step's event delivery, the Horror Event Director appends a new one on the nearest surviving part, or on the next Workpiece if the whole piece was scrapped (rule 13).
Defect log
If Any system appends a repair for an ignored_eventRefusedRefused (rule 11).
Topology
If A split is requested at boundary 0 or N (one side would be empty)RefusedRefused.
Topology
If A split leaves a half below N_bar_min or R_minAllowedAllowed, with a hard minimum of 1 cell per half. The floors in Formulas 1–2 apply at Create only. Each half keeps its cells exactly; a split never resamples, and no half is ever given a new count (rule 3). There is no other topology change that resamples.
Topology
If A split would take the Workpiece past parts_maxRefusedRefused before any change. Scrapped parts still count, because they stay in parts[].
Topology
If A part with rune strokes is splitAllowedEvery stroke sample goes with the half that holds its anchor cell. A stroke that crosses the cut is not truncated; its samples are divided between the halves, and its legibility is judged per half. A stroke whose samples all go to a Scrapped half is lost with it, as on a real blade.
Topology
If A join is requested on a part that is not Complete, or on a rivet anchor that is not punchedRefusedRefused. No defect is appended.
Topology
If A joined part is unjoinedAllowed + rivet_failThe Joint record is deleted, its slack with it. A rivet_fail entry is appended and stays. Unjoin is required for Ship and optional for the MVP.
Topology
If A part that belongs to a Joint is scrappedRefusedRefused. The player must unjoin first (which appends rivet_fail), so Living Armor and Grading never see a joint with a dead end.
Holes
If A cell is a hole (punched, or thickness at or below t_min)Partly refusedGrinding still increases material_removed and Heat keeps stepping. Deformation refuses to thicken it (Formula 6). Blood Runes refuses to cut a new stroke on it, and existing samples there count as illegible whatever their depth (Formula 7), so a torn plate cannot carry a readable rune.
Lifecycle
If No part can still satisfy the recipe's remaining steps (the player cut off too much, or burned through the last good metal)Never soft-locksThis is not a failure state. Recipes & Craft Session detects it and offers the two outs the Night Cycle supports: draw new stock, which ends this Workpiece as Scrapped through the Framework, or commit the piece as it is and accept the grade (Pillar 4). The night never soft-locks.
Lifecycle
If A Finished piece is reopened before commitBack to InWorkThe Framework moves it back to InWork. Heat sees the lifecycle change and clears its own thermal_settled (rule 9: the owner applies the effect); the Framework never writes it. After commit (Delivered or Mounted) there is no reopen.
Lifecycle
If A commit is requested while the piece is still coolingAllowedAllowed. Grading samples at the commit transition and the piece freezes as read-only; Heat stops stepping it.
Save
If A save is requested in the middle of a stepWaits for step endThe snapshot is taken only at a step boundary, after the Grading sample, so it is always consistent. A request mid-step waits for the end of that step.
Save
If A snapshot has a different schema versionRefused, untouchedAn older version goes through a registered migration; with no migration, or a newer version, the restore is refused and the live model is untouched. There is never a partial or best-effort load (rule 17 promises an exact restore). Night-Boundary Save decides the fallback.

Interactions

  • s_bar and N_bar_max together decide where the cap starts: the cap begins at s_bar · N_bar_max (64 cm by default). Lowering s_bar without raising N_bar_max makes long parts coarse, not fine.
  • s_plate and R_max work the same way per plate axis (48 cm by default).
  • parts_max × R_max² is the worst-case cell count (9 216 by default) and sets the performance and save budget. Raising either knob must be checked against the Heat Simulation frame budget.
  • d_legible_min matters only together with Blood Runes' own "rune holds" threshold on legible_fraction; tune them as a pair.
Not knobs
The quanta (1 °C, 1e-5 m, 1e-10 m²) are save and comparison constants owned by the future storage ADR. Changing them changes every recipe-derived count and every comparison, so they are not tunable.

08

Visual/Audio, Game Feel, UI

Visual/Audio requirements

None of its own. The model holds no rendering and no sound (rule 19). It exposes what presentation needs and nothing more:

  • Per-channel version counters and dirty ranges (rule 16), so the heat shader and the deformation mesh re-upload only what changed.
  • Per-face surface channels (scale, decarb, oxide, finish, ground depth), so a ground face and an unground face can look different.
  • The defect appended event, which Diegetic Audio and VFX may listen to (a crack that "sings"). What each defect looks and sounds like belongs to the writer's GDD and to the art bible.

Every visible mark must come from a channel or a log entry, so what the player sees on the piece is what Grading judges. The "mark that is not mine" (ignored_event) is drawn by the same rules as every other mark: no special glow, no unique effect (Pillar: Dread, Not Fright).

Game feel

Not applicable: the player never operates the model directly. Feel targets (hammer weight, response time) belong to Bar and Sheet Deformation and the Craft-Step Framework. The model's only feel duty is not to get in the way: a write is applied immediately (rule 8), so a strike's result is readable in the same step it lands.

UI requirements

None. The model has no screen and no HUD element. Displayed numbers (a temperature in the Order Sheet, for example) are quantised by Formula 9; the words used to describe the metal follow the Player Fantasy tone rules. Any inspection view belongs to Order Sheet & Diegetic UI.
